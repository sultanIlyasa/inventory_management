# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Warehouse staff and supervisors at TMMIN (Toyota Motor Manufacturing Indonesia). One unified UI covering all roles: floor counters who submit physical counts, admins who review discrepancy data, supervisors who download reports and track completion. Users range from digitally confident to digitally cautious; the interface must work for both without condescension. Primary context is a warehouse or office workstation during an active inventory period, often under mild time pressure.

## Product Purpose

Internal warehouse information system for managing daily material inputs, annual physical inventory counts (PID distribution, counting, discrepancy analysis), and warehouse monitoring (shortage/caution tracking, recovery days, overdue leaderboards). Success means warehouse staff can complete their assigned tasks with zero ambiguity, and supervisors can extract accurate reports without requiring IT support.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, official. The system earns trust through structure and consistency, not personality. Think of a well-organized binder: everything in its place, nothing surprising. Structured like a Notion database, focused like Linear, but more conservative and legible for users who aren't power-tool users.

## Anti-references

- Consumer SaaS dashboards with gradient hero metrics and animated numbers
- Dark-mode-as-aesthetic (no dark theme without a strong reason)
- Trendy glassmorphism, frosted cards, or blur effects
- Playful onboarding, tooltips-with-emoji, or informal copy
- Dense, small-text interfaces that feel like legacy ERP (the old extreme, also wrong)
- Any design that reads as "startup" or "tech product" rather than "enterprise internal tool"

## Design Principles

1. **Structure before style.** Tables, forms, and status indicators are the product. Visual treatment serves them; it never competes.
2. **Legibility at a glance.** A supervisor skimming 200 PID rows or a counter reading rack addresses under fluorescent light needs high contrast, generous sizing, and obvious hierarchy. Ambiguity costs real time.
3. **Predictable affordances.** Every interactive element behaves exactly as expected. No clever hover states, no hidden actions, no UI that requires discovery. If it's clickable, it looks clickable.
4. **Official without being cold.** The tone is institutional and reliable, but not hostile. Clear labels, honest status indicators, and sensible defaults communicate competence and care.
5. **Reduce cognitive load at task time.** During annual inventory or discrepancy review, users are focused on data accuracy. The UI should disappear — no noise, no decoration that pulls attention from the numbers.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Prioritize contrast ratios for text and interactive states. Support users who may not be fluent with modern web UI patterns: obvious focus states, large click targets, clear error messages with actionable guidance. No reliance on color alone to convey status.
